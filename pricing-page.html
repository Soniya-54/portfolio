Case study 01 · Programiz · May 2026

Pricing page redesign

Role

Creative Designer

Scope

Pricing page

Tools

Figma

Overview

People were visiting the pricing page, but too few were starting a trial. The plan prices and trial terms were hard to compare, so I simplified how they were presented and made the next step clearer.

The problem

The yearly plan showed $288 crossed out beside $9/month. Without clear context, that can make a legitimate offer feel misleading. Visitors had to work out the real annual cost before they could compare plans.

The page also had savings badges, several competing buttons, a money-back seal, and six different prices. All that detail distracted from the decision people came to make: which plan works for them?

What I changed

Made the full price clear

The yearly plan now shows $108 per year, so people can see the amount they will actually pay.

Removed crossed-out prices

A discount that is always there can make the original price feel made up. Removing it helped the offer feel more straightforward.

Put the saving beside the plan

I kept “Most Popular (Save $120)” with the plan. At $19 a month, the monthly plan costs $228 over 12 months. The $108 yearly plan is $120 less.

Led with the trial terms

I moved 7 days free. No charge today. Cancel anytime. higher on the page, where people could see the terms before choosing.

Gave both plans equal weight

The monthly option had a ghost button before. I made both calls to action equally clear, so either plan feels easy to choose.

What happened

After launch, the trial start rate rose from 1.31% to 1.55%. That is a 0.24 percentage-point increase, or about an 18% relative increase. Traffic was 26% lower during the same period. Programiz had also removed PRO banners from the main site.
