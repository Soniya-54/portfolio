Pricing page redesign

Role

Creative Designer

Scope

Pricing page

Tools

Figma, Framer

Type

Self-initiated

[Pricing page before / after]

Overview

The pricing page was getting traffic but underperforming on trial starts. I didn't have a brief for this — I looked at the page and diagnosed it myself.

The core problem: the page worked so hard to look like a deal that it had stopped looking trustworthy. I stripped it back, removed the fiction, and let the product speak for itself.

Result: trial start rate lifted 18% — on a colder, smaller audience than before.

The problem

The yearly plan showed $288 crossed out, replaced by $9/month. To a visitor reading it cold, a $288 product dropping to $9 reads as a dark pattern, not a discount.

Savings badges, competing CTAs, a money-back seal, and six numbers to mentally reconcile before deciding. The page was anxious. It didn't trust the product to sell itself.

Problem 01

Unbelievable strikethroughs

If every plan is always discounted, the original price is invented. Showing fiction costs trust.

Problem 02

Ambiguous pricing

$9/month billed as $108/year — users had to do maths to understand what they'd actually pay. They don't. They leave.

Problem 03

No clear next step

Equal weight on too many elements meant nothing felt like the obvious action.

What I changed

Showed the real billing amount

Yearly plan: $108/year. Not $9/month-when-you-squint.

Removed strikethrough prices

If we're always discounted, the original price is fiction. Removing it removes the trigger for skepticism.

Moved savings into the plan label

"Most Popular (Save $120)" — once, quietly, in context. Not shouting from a separate badge.

Rewrote the header to lead with the trial

7 days free. No charge today. Cancel anytime. — reassurance moved upstream, where the hesitation actually is.

Unified the CTAs

The old page used a ghost button for monthly — silently discouraging it. Both options should feel equally reachable.

What happened

Launch: May 21, 2026. Around the same time, Programiz removed PRO promotional banners from the main site — so the pricing page received less traffic and a colder audience. Despite that, trial start rate improved.

What I'd do next

The redesign removed friction but didn't address the plan split. 67% of career switchers choose yearly — there's room to design toward that choice more explicitly without reverting to the badge approach.

I'd also want to run this as a proper A/B test with equal traffic conditions to isolate the design's impact from the banner removal noise.
